# 1024 RPG — Type 02 Design Notes

## Status
**VERSION 1 · v0.3 POC TESTING — NOT FINAL**

Type 02 remains a City / Management experiment, but v0.3 deliberately changes the core hypothesis after v0.2 playtesting.

## POC history
### v0.1 — archived
Path: `type-02-v1-v0-1/`

Four building families existed directly on a swipe-based 4×4 board. Same-family + same-level merging caused excessive fragmentation and premature board lock.

### v0.2 — archived / comparison build
Path: `type-02-v1-v0-2/`

Used a single merge chain, 12 valid moves per quarter, development rights and a separate LIVE CITY. This solved the immediate board-lock issue and shortened the planning phase, but playtesting exposed a deeper issue: the 1024 interaction had too little variation, city feedback was weak, LIVE CITY was mostly passive, and the player made too few spatial decisions.

### v0.3 — current POC
Path: `type-02-v1-v0-3/`

New hypothesis:

> **The board itself is the city.**  
> **One 4×4 board = one city district.**  
> **Multiple completed districts = the final city.**

This experiment is inspired by placement/merge town-building puzzles, while retaining Type 02's own 1024-style level growth and city-management layer.

## v0.3 recommended rules being tested
1. **Direct placement instead of whole-board swipe.** The player sees the next planned building and chooses its exact empty cell.
2. **Four city families:** Residential, Industry / Commerce, Energy / Infrastructure, Public / Environment.
3. **Two matching orthogonal neighbors merge.** Placing next to the same family + same level consumes one matching neighbor and upgrades the placed building by one level. A new level can immediately chain again.
4. **16 placements per district** in this POC. This is a test value, not final balance.
5. **One reroll per district** lets the player reject the current next building once, creating a small but explicit planning resource.
6. **3×3 district city.** The run starts in the center district. Completing a district lets the player expand into an adjacent district. Old districts remain visible as permanent parts of the city.
7. **Nine completed districts end the run** and produce a city summary / score.
8. **City stats are global.** Residential, Industry, Energy and Public development across all districts feed Population, Finance, Energy and Happiness.

## Why v0.3 exists
v0.3 is not intended as a balance-complete game. It tests whether spatial ownership creates stronger player agency and feedback than v0.2's separate planning-board / LIVE-CITY structure.

Questions to validate:
- Does choosing the exact placement make each action meaningfully more interesting?
- Is two-tile adjacency merging intuitive and satisfying, or should a future build require three matching tiles?
- Is seeing completed districts persist in the 3×3 city map motivating?
- Does 16 placements make one district too short or too long?
- Should the next object be fully random, offered as a choice of 2–3 objects, or generated by city demand?
- Should roads, rivers, terrain, landmarks or blocked cells appear in later districts?
- Should adjacent districts affect each other at their borders?
- Should expansion direction itself create bonuses, terrain or strategic consequences?
- Is a nine-district run the right length?
- Which final score components should matter, and should there also be scenario objectives rather than only score?

## Deliberately NOT in v0.3 yet
- roads / traffic networks;
- terrain and rivers;
- special buildings and landmarks;
- disasters;
- district-to-district adjacency bonuses;
- scenario missions;
- meta-progression;
- save system;
- final economy / score balance.

These are withheld so the placement + merge + district-expansion loop can be judged first.

## Design principles
1. Type 02 must remain fundamentally different from Type 01.
2. Every important action should visibly change the city.
3. Placement should create spatial strategy rather than merely numerical accumulation.
4. Completed work should persist visually so the player feels ownership of the city.
5. POCs should isolate core questions before adding content depth.
6. Archived POCs remain playable for comparison and should not be overwritten.
